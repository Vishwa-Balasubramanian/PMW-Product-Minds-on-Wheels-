## 5. Problem Solving

**Problem 1: Data is messy and incomplete**
- Solution: Identified the 10 traps explicitly, explained why each matters, and designed the app to **show** rather than hide gaps. The evidence meter and conflict alerts turn weaknesses into strengths (transparency about uncertainty is a judging criterion).

**Problem 2: Too many cars, markets, models to demo in ~30 hours**
- Solution: Focus on 5 Series (G60) × USA as the primary demo (richest data: 4,000 comments, all 4 sources, 3 powertrains, 103 surveys, 78k volume). Switch to 7 Series × China as a secondary moment to show adaptability and honest evidence-level reporting. This keeps scope tight while proving scalability.

**Problem 3: Judges will expect a workflow diagram and audit trail, but they're boring static deliverables**
- Solution: Build them into the app itself. The audit timeline is a scrollable view on each requirement card; the workflow diagram is embedded in the feature tour or pitch deck, but the app demonstrates it live.

**Problem 4: Credibility of AI suggestions**
- Solution: Every suggestion must trace back to a real source (customer ID, survey score, web source). The one-click feature and the Challenge button enforce this. No ghost claims.

**Problem 5: How to distinguish from other teams' similar apps?**
- Solution: The five wow factors:
  1. "You already sell this" — match Wants to price lists and propose bundling or visibility (unique)
  2. Challenge button — AI argues against itself (trust-building, rare)
  3. One-click to quotes — instant source verification
  4. Evidence meter — visual confidence level (transparent, honest)
  5. Conflict alerts — where sources disagree is surfaced
